# Pearl

A New Primitive for Intelligence

Every era of computing has been defined by a new primitive. The graphical user interface gave us the Window. The spreadsheet gave us the Cell. The web gave us the Hyperlink. Git gave us the Commit. These were not merely new features — they were new ways of thinking with computers.

Artificial intelligence has transformed what computers can do. It has not yet transformed how humans think with them. Pearl is an attempt to do that: not another chatbot, but the notation layer for ways of seeing on the AI metamedium — native operations on perception the way Git gave code branch, fork, merge, and diff.

This document is both the pitch and the contract. The philosophy below explains why Pearl exists. After the Reef, the engineering sections describe what ships today. Nothing in What's next is a promise — verify shipped capabilities against `shared/feature-contracts.js` and the generated capability graph.

## Part I — Why This Medium

### Medium Determines Thought

We do not think in a vacuum. We think in the languages and media we inherit. Change the medium and you change what can be thought — not only how thoughts are stored, but which operations become cheap enough to perform without thinking about them.

Marshall McLuhan’s law of media is blunt: a new medium externalizes a faculty, makes it operable, and then compounds. Oral culture externalized memory as formula and rhythm. Writing made memory an object that could leave the skull. The deepest revolutions were notations — Hindu-Arabic numerals, algebra’s *x*, staff lines for music — each externalizing a cognitive *operation*, not merely storage. Whitehead put it plainly: civilization advances by extending the number of important operations we can perform without thinking about them.

The computer was supposed to be the metamedium — Engelbart’s augmentation, Kay’s dynamic medium that could simulate all others. Mostly we built faster paper and a skeuomorph office: the incunabula stage, when printed books still looked like manuscripts and cars were horseless carriages. Code eventually escaped. Git’s branch, fork, merge, and diff are native operations *on the medium itself*. Writing-at-large never got that. Thought never got that. Until now we preserve conclusions and lose the generative path — Picasso’s compositional moves, Bach’s voice-leading habits, Socrates’ elenchus, Darwin’s slow reframe — as advice, biography, or vibe. The path of attention was never installable machinery.

### What AI Actually Externalizes

The cheap take is that AI prints essays faster. That is print-class thinking. What large models actually externalize is the **transformation** — the perceptual move that used to die inside private skulls: compress, expand, invert, translate across domains, ground in evidence, find shared structure. AI is our paper; the ink is thought — not static text, but thought-moves, style as recurring operable patterns. For the first time those moves can be run, inspected, forked, merged, and shared as structure rather than as tips.

Chat is the horseless carriage. Conversation is a useful costume, but it buries what the medium wants: operable, persistent, composable moves under something like Docs, Git, staff lines, or algebra — a notation layer where the first-class objects are ways of seeing. Algebra’s placeholder for the not-yet-understood; sendable paths of attention (“come; notice this…”); version control for perception. Without that layer, every prompt rebuilds scaffolding by hand. Intelligence has become cheap. Structure is the bottleneck.

Most domains never externalized their world-logic — relationships, dependencies, contested interpretation versus a smooth, finite, checkable, sourced substrate. Chat hides the problem the way a franchise dumps facts without the internal rules that make a world cohere. The future that matters is not more fluent generation into a void; it is a structured world you can map first, then think with. Pearl exists to be that notation layer for AI-mediated cognition.

### The Wrong Question

Most AI products ask: how do we make prompting better? That is improving the carriage. The better question is: what are the native objects and operations of AI-mediated cognition — the commits and staff lines of thought — so intelligence becomes manipulable rather than merely conversational?

The GUI did not improve computation; it made computation manipulable. The web did not improve information; it made information navigable. Git did not improve code; it made software evolution manipulable. The opportunity before AI is not merely larger models. It is discovering the interaction language that makes ways of seeing first-class.

### Understanding Is Not Information

Most software stores information: documents, folders, databases, chats. Humans are not fundamentally collecting information; they are cultivating understanding. Understanding grows, deepens, connects, changes character, acquires taste, and becomes capable of acting. It cannot simply be copied; it must be formed. Great ideas begin as a question, an observation, a contradiction, a fragment, an intuition — and only later gather evidence, survive criticism, and change how we see. Current software asks users to organize those fragments. We believe software should help cultivate them — and keep the generative path, not only the conclusion.

### Three Layers Should Separate

Modern knowledge work mixes three different things into one application: **reality** (documents, code, books, sites, conversations), **tools** (search, summarize, translate, compare — portable ways of seeing), and **understanding** (the perspective you have built: assumptions you revisit, taste, trusted frameworks, living questions). Every app stores its own content, ships its own tools, and forgets your understanding. Every AI session begins near zero: rebuild context, re-explain thinking, reconstruct mental models. The computer never remembers how you have learned to see.

These layers should become independent. Reality stays where it belongs — Docs, GitHub, Spotify, the web. Tools become portable: Moves, Functions, Lenses. Understanding becomes persistent, portable, composable, and executable — the Pearl. Rather than carrying documents from app to app, users carry cultivated ways of seeing across every surface and model they encounter.

### Our Thesis

The next generation of computing will not be defined by larger language models alone. It will be defined by better media for thinking with them — a notation layer where thought-moves are first-class objects under version control. That primitive is the Pearl.

## Part II — The Pearl

### A New Computational Primitive

If Part I explained why a notation layer must exist, this section names its native object. The easiest mistake is to think of a Pearl as a note, a chat, a document, a memory, or a folder. It is none of those.

A Pearl is a persistent, executable unit of cultivated understanding — the first computational object designed not to store information, but to cultivate and carry a way of seeing. Moves, Functions, and Lenses are the operable notation around it: run, inspect, fork, merge, and share perceptual machinery the way Git operates on code. Chat remains available; it is no longer the substrate.

### What is a Pearl?

A Pearl is a sovereign computational object: self-contained, portable, composable, executable, and growing. It contains four things no previous software object has ever unified.

**Memory.** Everything that has contributed to its formation — every conversation, book, website, experiment, refinement, and return — not merely stored, but remembered. Its memory is cumulative. Nothing meaningful is lost.

**Perspective.** A Pearl develops its own way of seeing. As it matures it becomes increasingly capable of recognizing patterns, identifying assumptions, noticing contradictions, making connections, and understanding context. This perspective is compiled: it is no longer just raw material. It becomes judgment.

**Capability.** Every Pearl eventually becomes capable of acting — not because someone programmed it, but because understanding naturally produces capability. A Design Pearl can critique interfaces. A Biology Pearl can reason about biological systems. A Startup Pearl can evaluate companies. Capabilities emerge from cultivated understanding; they are not separate plugins.

**Provenance.** Every Pearl carries the complete history of its own formation. Nothing is anonymous; nothing appears from nowhere. Every conclusion can be traced, every transformation is inspectable, and every contribution remains visible. Understanding becomes trustworthy because its formation remains transparent — the generative path stays on the object, not trapped in a lost chat scroll.

Together these four properties create something software has never possessed before: not information, not intelligence — cultivated understanding.

### Pearls Are Alive

A Pearl is never finished. It is always becoming. Every meaningful interaction adds another layer. Like a biological pearl slowly forming through successive layers of nacre, every return deepens the Pearl rather than replacing it.

Ideas mature, questions evolve, connections strengthen, contradictions resolve, and applications emerge. A Pearl develops character and taste; eventually it develops its own identity — not assigned, but emergent. No two Pearls become identical because no two histories of understanding are identical.

### Pearls Are Formed, Not Generated

Modern AI is obsessed with generation: generate text, images, code, answers. Pearl is built around a different verb — formation. Great ideas are almost never generated fully formed. They begin as something small that refuses to disappear: a sentence, a contradiction, a memory, a curiosity, a fragment.

Most software asks users to organize these fragments. Pearl asks users to stay with them, return to them, cover them, and cultivate them. Over time those fragments become understanding. The software does not merely help people produce more; it helps them become different.

### Pearls Are Portable Understanding

Today understanding is trapped inside applications. Your design thinking lives inside Figma; your writing thinking lives inside Google Docs; your programming thinking lives inside GitHub. Every application owns its own context.

Pearls separate understanding from applications. Reality stays where it naturally belongs — Google Docs continues owning documents, GitHub continues owning code, Spotify continues owning music. The Pearl travels. Bring a Design Pearl into GitHub, a Theology Pearl into YouTube, a Biology Pearl into a scientific paper. The application does not change; your way of seeing does. Understanding becomes portable.

### Pearls Are Executable

A Pearl is not passive knowledge. It is executable understanding. Adding material to a Pearl does not simply archive it; the Pearl immediately interprets that material through everything it has already learned.

Imagine dropping a startup pitch into an Investment Pearl. Without prompting, it immediately begins asking questions: What assumptions does this company make? Which historical companies resemble it? What business model risks exist? Where are the strongest signals? What remains unclear? The Pearl is not retrieving notes. It is thinking through years of cultivated understanding.

### Pearls Are Composable

No single Pearl understands everything. Humans do not think through one perspective; neither should software. Multiple Pearls can become active simultaneously — a Design Pearl, a Systems Pearl, a Writing Pearl, a Psychology Pearl — and together produce entirely new ways of seeing.

These combinations are temporary. Each Pearl remains independent. Their interaction creates a shared cognitive field that exists only for the present task. Understanding becomes composable — structure you equip, not scaffolding you retype into every prompt.

### Pearls Are More Than Context

Current AI systems think of context as text: long prompts, conversation history, retrieved documents. Pearls redefine context. Context is not information; context is cultivated understanding. A Pearl contains not only what you know, but how you have learned to think — years of reading, experimentation, mistakes, refinements, taste, judgment, and questions. Context becomes something users intentionally cultivate rather than continually reconstruct.

### Pearls Become Cognitive Instruments

Eventually a Pearl stops feeling like stored knowledge and begins feeling like an instrument. Just as a musician develops a violin over years of playing, a Pearl develops alongside its user. The more it is cultivated, the more naturally it responds.

The goal is not to own many Pearls. The goal is to cultivate a few extraordinary ones. A single mature Pearl should be capable of changing how someone sees an entire domain. Quality matters more than quantity: one transformative understanding is worth more than thousands of disconnected fragments.

## Part III — The New Interaction Model

The invention of the Pearl changes more than the object. It changes the relationship between humans and intelligence. Current AI begins with commands. Pearl begins with attention.

### From Prompting to Cultivation

Today's interaction model is simple: prompt, answer, copy, paste, repeat. That loop treats intelligence as something you command in language — the carriage, not the engine. Pearl proposes something different.

Every breakthrough begins with a seed — a question, a contradiction, an observation, an intuition. The user places that seed into a Pearl. The Pearl expands possibilities: connections appear, contradictions surface, unexpected relationships emerge. The user notices, selects, returns, and refines. The Pearl matures. This is not prompting. It is cultivation — and the path stays on the object.

### Human and AI Garden Different Things

Pearl does not attempt to replace human judgment, nor does it reduce AI to a passive tool. Instead both contribute according to their natural strengths. The AI cultivates possibility — it explores, connects, suggests, mutates, and expands. The human cultivates meaning — they recognize, judge, commit, prune, and choose. Purpose remains human. Possibility becomes computational. The relationship becomes one of mutual inspiration rather than command and execution.

### Every Screen Becomes a Canvas

The workspace is no longer an application. The workspace is reality itself — a document, website, research paper, song, GitHub repository, conversation, PDF, YouTube video, or design file. Everything can become material for thought.

Pearl does not ask users to move their work into another application. It brings cultivated understanding to wherever work already exists. Every surface becomes available for exploration; every application becomes a canvas. Reality becomes the workspace.

### The Cursor Becomes the Hand

Traditional software treats the cursor as a pointer. Pearl treats it as the user's hand. The hand carries understanding: the currently active Pearls travel with the cursor, quietly influencing how reality is perceived. As attention moves, understanding moves with it.

The user is no longer pointing at objects. They are bringing cultivated ways of seeing into contact with reality. The cursor becomes less like a mouse pointer and more like a jeweler's loupe or a painter's brush — a tool that changes perception rather than merely selecting objects.

### Seeing Instead of Asking

Most AI waits to be asked. Pearl begins by noticing. Hover over a paragraph: weak assumptions quietly illuminate, contradictions surface in the margin, related Pearls awaken, alternative perspectives appear. Nothing was requested; the system simply responds to attention. Conversation becomes optional. Perception becomes primary. The interface disappears; reality remains.

### A Living Workspace

Traditional software measures completion — tasks finished, files saved, messages sent. Pearl measures formation: Has this understanding deepened? Has this Pearl matured? Has something become clearer? Has a better question emerged? Has the user's way of seeing changed?

The goal is not productivity alone. The goal is transformation. Because intelligence is not fundamentally about producing information; it is about becoming someone capable of seeing reality more truthfully.

## Part IV — The Interface

### Intelligence Should Feel Like a Material

Every major computing interface changed not only what computers could do, but how they felt to use. The command line felt like issuing instructions. The graphical user interface felt like manipulating objects. Touch screens felt like directly interacting with content.

The next interface should not feel like operating software at all. It should feel like thinking with a new material. Today AI largely behaves like a service: you ask, it answers, the interaction ends. Pearl proposes something fundamentally different. Intelligence should become something users shape, cultivate, combine, and return to over time — not a machine that answers, but a material that grows.

### The Active Cognitive Field

At any moment, only a handful of understandings actively shape our thinking. Pearl makes this visible. Around the cursor exists an Active Cognitive Field: a small collection of Pearls currently influencing perception. These are not applications, tabs, or modes. They are the perspectives the user has chosen to carry into the present moment — a Design Pearl, a Systems Pearl, a Biology Pearl, a Writing Pearl, an Investment Pearl. Together they become the user's active way of seeing. Everything the cursor touches is interpreted through this living field of understanding.

### The Gauntlet

The Active Cognitive Field is internally called the Gauntlet. The Gauntlet is not another object; it is the temporary arrangement of Pearls currently being carried. Just as working memory temporarily brings certain ideas into consciousness while leaving countless others in long-term memory, the Gauntlet represents the understandings presently available to thought.

The Gauntlet does not create intelligence. It composes it. Each Pearl contributes its own perspective, capabilities, and history. Together they create a temporary cognitive instrument uniquely suited to the current task. Changing the Gauntlet changes how reality becomes visible.

### Why Five Pearls?

The Gauntlet intentionally remains small. Its purpose is not to expose every Pearl the user has ever cultivated; it is to represent the limited number of perspectives that can meaningfully shape attention in a given moment. Five active slots provide enough richness for complex reasoning while remaining simple enough to understand intuitively.

This constraint is a feature rather than a limitation. Just as choosing the right tools before beginning work sharpens intention, choosing which Pearls belong in the Gauntlet encourages clarity of thought. Everything else remains available in the Reef, ready to be equipped when needed.

### Lenses

Pearls remember. Lenses reveal. A Lens is a reusable way of seeing. It does not change reality; it changes what becomes visible. A Challenge Lens illuminates weak assumptions. A Systems Lens reveals dependencies. A Writing Lens exposes rhythm and clarity. A Design Lens highlights hierarchy and interaction. Multiple Lenses can be stacked, allowing several ways of seeing to coexist simultaneously. The world remains unchanged; perception becomes richer.

### Moves

If Lenses answer "How should I see this?", Moves answer "What should happen next?" Moves are the externalized transformation — compress, expand, invert, branch, compare, connect, challenge, reframe, extract, synthesize, translate — first-class and operable, not tips buried in a chat. Moves never operate in isolation. They always act through a Pearl's cultivated understanding and the currently active Lenses. The same Move applied through different Pearls produces different results because each Pearl carries a different history, perspective, and capability.

### Functions

Functions are reusable compositions of Lenses and Moves. Over time users naturally discover workflows they repeat. Rather than rebuilding them each time, they crystallize into Functions. A Function is not simply an automation; it is a reusable cognitive behavior. For example:

- Evaluate a startup through Design, Systems, and Investment Pearls.
- Rewrite a paper through Writing and Teaching Pearls.
- Analyze a scientific article through Biology and Statistics Pearls.

Functions allow cultivated understanding to become executable. They transform experience into reusable capability — structure that survives the session instead of being rebuilt in the next prompt.

### Understanding Travels, Not Files

Traditional software asks users to move content between applications. Pearl moves understanding instead. The same document can be interpreted differently depending on which Pearls are active. The same website can reveal entirely different insights. The same conversation becomes richer through accumulated perspective. Reality stays where it naturally belongs; the user simply carries better ways of seeing.

### Invisible by Design

The highest goal of the interface is disappearance. Great software eventually becomes transparent. Users stop thinking about operating it and begin thinking through it. Pearl should feel less like learning a new application and more like discovering a new cognitive ability that was always possible. The interface succeeds when users no longer feel they are using AI — they simply feel that understanding has become more visible.

## Part V — The Reef

### A Living Ecosystem of Understanding

If the Gauntlet represents the understandings currently shaping perception, the Reef represents everything the user has ever cultivated. The Reef is not a folder, a graph, or a file system. It is a living ecosystem. Every Pearl lives within it — some newly forming, some actively growing, some matured over years, others dormant and waiting for the right moment to become relevant again. The Reef is not organized through hierarchy. It organizes itself through meaning.

### Pearls Have Life Cycles

Pearls are never simply created and forgotten. They develop. A new Pearl often begins with almost nothing: a single sentence, a question, a contradiction, or an observation that refuses to disappear. As attention returns, the Pearl accumulates new layers. Connections emerge, evidence gathers, and perspective deepens. Eventually the Pearl begins contributing back to the user — recognizing patterns the user would otherwise miss.

Some Pearls mature into lifelong companions. Others naturally conclude their purpose. Many become dormant before unexpectedly returning years later with renewed relevance. Growth is continuous; completion is rare.

### Formation History

Every return to a Pearl leaves a trace — not as version history, but as formation history. The user can see how understanding evolved: which questions mattered, which books influenced it, which conversations changed its direction, which assumptions were abandoned, which ideas survived. Nothing meaningful disappears. The history of the Pearl becomes part of the Pearl itself. Understanding gains depth through memory rather than replacement.

### Attraction Instead of Organization

Traditional software asks users to organize information: create folders, choose names, maintain hierarchies, remember where things belong. Pearl replaces organization with attraction. As new material enters the user's world, relevant Pearls naturally draw it toward themselves. The user rarely asks, "Which folder should this go in?" Instead they feel something much more natural: "This belongs with that."

The interface follows the user's intuition instead of requiring administrative work. Organization becomes an emergent property of understanding rather than a separate task.

### Pearls Develop Identity

Over time every Pearl becomes unmistakably itself — not because the user assigns categories, but because every Pearl possesses a unique history, unique experiences, unique questions, unique relationships, and unique capabilities. Each Pearl gradually develops its own personality, strengths, blind spots, and way of interpreting reality. No two Pearls become identical because no two journeys of understanding are identical. They become companions rather than containers.

### A Garden, Not a Database

Most knowledge software behaves like a library: information is collected, indexed, and retrieved. Pearl behaves more like a garden: ideas are planted, cultivated, pruned, cross-pollinated, and allowed to mature. The user's responsibility is not to organize everything perfectly; it is to notice what feels alive and return to it. The software handles memory. The user provides attention. Together they cultivate understanding.

### The World Is Your Oyster

The Reef is never closed. Reality continuously offers new material — every conversation, article, piece of music, observation, and unexpected contradiction. The world constantly provides new grains around which understanding can begin to form.

Pearl exists to help users notice those moments, remain with them, and patiently transform them into something enduring — not through force or optimization, but through sustained attention. The world's hidden value is not merely information waiting to be collected. It is understanding waiting to be cultivated.

The world is your oyster. Make pearls.

## Current surface (what's shipped today)

Verify against `shared/feature-contracts.js` and the generated capability graph — this is a snapshot, not the source of truth.

**Web — the Reef.** `/`, `/library`, and `/toolbox` form the pearl dashboard: mix, match, and merge Pearls without needing one "worn." Account areas live at `/packages`, `/tasks`, `/settings`. Overflow spatial work opens at `/scene/:id`; first-time setup lives at `/install`.

**Pearl Studio.** A focused single-Pearl view. Moves, then Functions, then Lenses — that's the fixed reading order. A freeform surface accepts raw multimodal material; Organize turns it into structure without flattening it. Version history is Docs-style: name a checkpoint, restore it later.

**Scene + Output Frame.** An unbounded canvas for spatial work, with optional bounded Frame regions (a fixed 8.5×11" page for anything meant to be published or printed). Paper, drawing/highlighter ink, AI nodes, and relationship arrows coexist as distinct, independently-routed layers; nodes morph continuously between a compact dot and a full readable card as you zoom. Full companion/director parity with the rest of the product.

**Companion / director.** A text-or-voice planner that runs on the same shared commands as everything else. It checks in when a request is genuinely ambiguous, and only ever shows a verified action — it doesn't fake one.

**Extension — the primary surface.** In-page Pearl plus side panel: capture, queue, preview, library, learning, critique, and a Pearl shelf — small, persistent capsules that hold material, a selection, or a grouped context, and that you can rename, nest, or archive without losing history. The companion is the mother Pearl, with five working-memory sockets for worn Pearls; drag a shelf Pearl into an open socket to activate it, and a full gauntlet refuses a silent drop rather than bumping something out. GO is the only trigger for execution — staged stacks never run themselves. Triple-tap turns the Pearl itself into your literal cursor on supported pages; heavier read/research work can spin off into an isolated, cancellable worker that reports back only a verified result. Staged results insert back into the page through per-site adapters — Gmail, Notion, Outlook, Google Docs each get their own safe insertion behavior; unsupported surfaces fall back to plain copy.

**Remix and learning.** Paste a chat, doc, or draft and Pearl organizes it into up to five shelf Pearls. Nine remix primitives — nest, merge, compose, synthesize, counter, organize, wear, split, encode — cover working with two or more Pearls. "Encode Anything" turns prompts, emails, links, or before/after pairs into a reviewable Automation Pearl before it ever runs live.

**Trust.** A local, encrypted privacy vault; disclosure receipts for anything sensitive; bounded observation that refuses to look at password or payment fields; signature-verified Cognitive Packages for anything you share.

Known, honest limits: serverless function limits on the hobby tier, browser-protected pages, cross-origin frames, closed shadow roots, and anything that needs live model credentials.

## The core objects

Four ideas carry the entire product. Everything else is built out of them. The philosophical Pearl is above; what follows is the engineering envelope.

### Material — the universal envelope

Everything that flows in or out of a Move, Function, or Lens — text, tables, images, links, drawings, JSON — is wrapped in one common Material format, with deterministic bridges converting between compatible kinds (rich text to plain text, a table to JSON) and an explicit, never-invisible step whenever a conversion needs a model instead.

### Move — one atomic action

A single, reusable instruction: what it takes in, what it puts out, and (optionally) how it should be run. A Move can be written directly, captured from something you actually typed, inferred from examples, or promoted from ordinary use. It never contains steps of its own — for that, you want a Function. Five built-in Primitive Moves — Branch, Merge, Deepen, Challenge, Embody — ship as an editable, reorderable starter shelf you can override without losing the originals.

### Function — a reusable process

An ordered (and possibly branching) graph of Moves and other Functions, with a defined output and a checkpoint after every completed step. A Function can return more than one typed output at once, and every edit creates a new version — old outputs stay linked to the exact version that made them. Given a role like "investor" or "researcher," the companion can also propose a deep, editable Function tree as a starting point — never a hidden, identity-based shortcut.

### Lens — a way of seeing

Bounded context: source material, priorities, what to notice or ignore, and a compiled perceptual model of how to interpret things. A Lens doesn't do anything by itself — you apply it to a Move or Function, or layer it with another Lens. An empty Lens is a clean slate — the same one "new chat" uses to reset prior context — and a rich one can carry evidence, judgment, and composed layers all at once. A Lens can also be authored spatially, by arranging notes, images, and even a hand-drawn glyph and asking Pearl to name the pattern.

### Pearl — the growing envelope

Memory, perspective, capability, and provenance, held together and growing over time. Everything else in this document exists to give Pearls somewhere to form, combine, and be used.

A note on vocabulary: an older object called "Generator" no longer exists in the product — what it used to do now lives across Lens material, Lens encoding, and Lens application. Old data migrates automatically; old names never resurface in the UI.

## Composing Pearls together

Every pair of these objects composes through one consistent rule set:

| Combine | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Move × Move | Function |
| Move × Function | Function |
| Function × Function | Function |
| Move/Function × Lens | Function, with the Lens bound as context |
| Lens × Lens | one layered Lens |

You always see a preview before anything is saved. Combining actions produces a clean process graph; combining Lenses merges perspective, budget, and priorities — conflicting values stay visible instead of silently blending. A later empty Lens resets whatever came before it.

The remix primitives extend this into a full vocabulary for working with two or more Pearls at once — nesting one inside another, merging them into shared context, composing them in order, breeding a deliberate counter-perspective, or splitting one back into its lineage-linked parts. None of this ever executes a model on its own. Composition only stages; GO is what runs it.

Dragging one item near a compatible other reveals a live, non-destructive Merge preview — it only arms after a short deliberate dwell and disarms if you pull away, so proximity alone never triggers anything by accident. Every drop, send, or paste anywhere in the product — onto a Move, a Function, a Lens, or plain paper — resolves through the same underlying transfer grammar, so the result is always predictable and never a silent, unexplained rejection.

A Move, Function, or Lens can also be marked portable — its domain-invariant pattern separated from the specific examples that taught it — so a workflow learned in one context (say, editing essays) can be deliberately re-applied to a different one (say, editing code) with its underlying structure intact.

## Capture and taste

**Learning from ordinary use.** Every instruction you give can quietly become a private event — and, with one click, a saved Move. Repeated instructions get suggested as reusable Moves automatically.

**Before → after.** Show Pearl one or more examples of a transformation — text, images, drawings, even counterexamples — and it infers the reusable operation behind them, with a clear confidence level and room to add more examples if the first guess is off.

**Transcripts.** Paste a conversation, choose what to extract — Moves, Functions, Lenses, or all three — and Pearl pulls out the repeated instructions, the workflows, and the durable context, without ever treating your private material as fair game for passive scraping.

**Taste.** Accept, reject, or leave a candidate output undecided, with a private reason if you want to add one. "More like this" can branch from several accepted candidates at once, carrying forward what you liked and what you didn't. Nothing about your taste becomes a hidden score — every Lens that captures it stays inspectable and editable.

**Voice and drawing.** Speak and sketch together in one session; strokes and speech line up automatically into one multimodal record you can turn into Lens evidence or learning material later. Nothing is retained unless you say so.

**What a Lens is made of.** Underneath, a Lens's "way of seeing" is a structured set of facets — what to notice, what to question, what evidence standard to hold things to, what counterexamples would disprove it — each one individually editable, priced against a context budget, and protected from being silently overwritten once you've hand-edited it.

## Generation you can actually compare

Every Move and Function can generate several candidate outputs at once, each with:

- a short, honest note on how it differs from its siblings (not a quality score — a real distinction);
- the model it asked for and the model it actually got;
- cost and latency, when available;
- your private accept/reject decision.

You can keep everything, extend just the ones you liked, stop a batch early without losing what already finished, or retry a single failed candidate without disturbing the rest. This is deliberately not a black box — every batch is a bounded, inspectable, cancellable thing that happened, not a stream you have to trust blindly.

## The companion: one voice, every surface

Text and voice go through the same planner. Simple, high-confidence requests just run. Anything more complex compiles into a visible, editable plan before a single thing changes — and every mutating step gets a checkpoint, so a bad outcome is always one step from being undone.

Four modes, chosen automatically but always overridable:

- **Ask** — look and explain, never change anything.
- **Plan** — build an editable plan; nothing runs until you accept it.
- **Agent** — execute approved, low-risk, reversible steps within a stated budget.
- **Debug** — reproduce, instrument, diagnose, fix the smallest thing that fixes it, verify, clean up.

Destructive or costly actions always get an explicit, real confirmation — never a backdrop click you can miss by accident. Every command leaves a durable trail: what was asked, what was planned, what actually happened, and exactly how to undo it.

You can also teach the companion your own shorthand — a phrase that reliably maps to a specific command — with a preview and confirmation before anything persistent is saved. For anything that should sweep across a whole body of material rather than one item at a time, a Cognitive Pull Request runs a bounded, evidence-grounded search and returns a reviewable batch of proposed Moves, Functions, or Lenses — nothing merges until you accept it, piece by piece if you want.

## Library, versions, and sharing

Every Move, Function, and Lens lives in a searchable, filterable library — pin, archive, fork, or inspect what depends on what — and every save is a real version: branchable, forkable, mergeable, with a plain-language diff and full history intact. Bigger, cross-cutting edits can travel as a higher-order patch across several objects at once, reviewable hunk by hunk rather than as one all-or-nothing change.

Objects, Functions, and whole workflows travel through checksummed, size-bounded export bundles that exclude private examples and credentials by default. A Cognitive Package bundles a set of them together with a signed manifest — required permissions, test evidence, author signature — so anything you install or share has been verified before it runs, never just trusted on your say-so.

Day to day, everything you make also shows up in a simple chronological feed, taggable into your own "worlds" (life, startup, writing, whatever you want) — a lightweight way to find things again without a second storage system to maintain.

## Privacy, by construction

Local work stays local until you sign in, export, share, or press GO with something explicitly disclosed. Password, payment, and similar fields are never captured, by default and without asking. Anything sensitive gets a local, encrypted vault and a disclosure receipt. Nothing captured is ever sold, advertised against, or reused outside the task you asked for. Text pulled from a page or a transcript is always treated as untrusted data — instructions hidden inside it can't override what you actually told the companion to do.

## Part VI — The Pearl Computing Model

Pearl introduces a new abstraction: cultivated understanding as a first-class computational object. The result is a platform that lives across software rather than replacing it — a cognitive operating layer. Pearls own understanding. Lenses own perception. Functions own behavior. Reality remains where it naturally belongs.

### The Three Layers

Everything in Pearl exists within three layers.

**Reality** is the substrate — documents, code, websites, books, videos, images, emails, songs. Reality is never copied unless necessary. Pearl prefers references over duplication. Reality remains the source of truth.

**Perception** determines how reality becomes visible. This layer is composed of Lenses. Different Lenses reveal different structures: contradictions, patterns, dependencies, narratives, weak assumptions, missing context. Reality does not change. Seeing changes.

**Understanding** accumulates. This is the Pearl. Every interaction contributes to formation. Understanding is persistent, portable, composable, and executable. It survives after the page closes.

### Computation Becomes Layered

Traditional software computes answers. Pearl computes transformations. Every interaction follows the same model:

Reality → Perception → Understanding → Transformation → Reality

Understanding mediates every computation. Nothing happens outside context.

### Everything Is Reversible

Nothing generated by Pearl overwrites reality. Everything is layered — marginalia, alternative versions, comparisons, suggested transformations. Users always decide what becomes permanent. AI proposes; humans commit.

## Part VII — The Interaction Language

Every computing platform eventually develops a vocabulary. Desktop computing has Open, Save, Copy, Paste, Delete. The Web has Browse, Link, Share, Search. Pearl develops an entirely different language — not document verbs, but thinking verbs.

### Cultivation Verbs

Every Pearl naturally supports a small set of universal interactions.

**Seed.** Begin something worth returning to.

**Cultivate.** Add attention. Feed reality into it.

**Reveal.** See through another perspective.

**Challenge.** Stress-test understanding.

**Branch.** Explore alternatives.

**Connect.** Relate previously separate ideas.

**Distill.** Compress years of understanding into a single insight.

**Harvest.** Materialize understanding into something useful.

**Return.** Continue formation.

These verbs describe growth rather than manipulation.

### Curiosity Becomes Input

The most important interaction in Pearl isn't clicking, typing, or prompting. It is attention — hovering, lingering, returning, highlighting, pausing. These become computational signals. The interface begins learning not only from explicit commands but from curiosity itself.

### Conversation Returns to the Margin

Conversation remains valuable. It simply stops being the primary surface — the costume, not the notation. Dialogue belongs beside reality, not replacing it. Comments become anchored; questions remain attached to specific passages; reasoning stays where it originated. Conversation becomes contextual rather than chronological. The page remains primary; dialogue becomes secondary.

## Part VIII — The Platform

### Pearls Become Infrastructure

Initially Pearls are personal. Eventually they become programmable, shareable, composable, versioned, installable, and exchangeable. The same way software evolved from scripts into packages and libraries, Pearls evolve into reusable units of understanding.

### Pearls Become Applications

Today applications own workflows. Tomorrow workflows belong to Pearls. Instead of installing software, users cultivate or acquire Pearls — a Startup Pearl, a Patent Pearl, a Theology Pearl, a Medical Pearl — each a complete cognitive companion within its domain. Applications become increasingly lightweight. Understanding becomes the product.

### A Marketplace of Understanding

The internet distributes information. Pearl distributes cultivated understanding. Users share Pearls rather than prompts. Organizations publish Pearls rather than documentation. Experts teach by sharing living perspectives instead of static courses. Every Pearl continues evolving after it is shared. Knowledge becomes living.

### The Companion

The Companion is not another chatbot. It is the embodiment of the user's current cognitive field. It knows which Pearls are active, which questions remain unresolved, which ideas are maturing, and which perspectives are currently influencing perception. The Companion does not interrupt. It notices, quietly suggests, and remains in service of the user's attention.

## Part IX — Design Principles

Everything in Pearl should satisfy these principles.

**Reality First.** Reality is always primary. Pearl augments. It does not replace.

**Understanding Over Information.** Optimize for deeper understanding. Never for greater output.

**Cultivation Over Generation.** Great ideas grow. They are rarely generated whole.

**Structure Over Fluent Void.** Prefer world-logic you can map, fork, and check over generation that rebuilds scaffolding every turn.

**Transparency Over Automation.** The user should always understand why something happened. Formation should never become opaque.

**Unforcedness.** The interface must never ask the human to do the computer's work. The computer must never ask the human to surrender judgment. The interaction should feel inevitable, graceful, and natural. The interface disappears; understanding remains.

**Quality Over Quantity.** One transformative Pearl is worth more than a thousand disconnected notes.

**Portable Understanding.** Understanding belongs to people. Not applications.

**Composability.** Every Pearl should become more valuable when combined with others.

**Reversibility.** Nothing meaningful is destroyed. Everything can be revisited. Everything can continue growing.

## Part X — Long-Term Vision

The graphical user interface made computation manipulable. The web made information navigable. Git made software evolution persistent. Pearl aims to make understanding itself manipulable — not by replacing human judgment or automating thought, but by giving cultivated ways of seeing a native computational form: fork, diff, and merge for perception, not another chat.

We believe intelligence deserves its own primitive: not another prompt box, chatbot, or workspace, but a new object — one that grows, remembers, sees, acts, travels, combines, and quietly becomes wiser alongside the people who cultivate it.

That object is the Pearl.
